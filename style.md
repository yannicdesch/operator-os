## Voice in three lines

- [e.g. Direct. Short sentences. Then a longer one when it earns its place.]
- [e.g. Concrete examples over abstractions.]
- [e.g. First person. Active voice. I decided, not "it was decided."]

## Words and phrases I never use

- [e.g. leverage, seamless, synergy, game-changer, "circle back"]
- [e.g. "I hope this email finds you well"]
- [e.g. exclamation marks in business writing]

## Words and framings I do use

- [e.g. "ship" not "launch"; "customers" not "users" when talking to the board]
- [e.g. I name the decision in the first line.]

## Formats

### Email
- Length: [e.g. under 120 words unless it's a decision memo]
- Structure: [e.g. ask or decision first, context second, next step last]
- Sign-off: [e.g. just my first name]

### Slack / Teams
- [e.g. no greetings, one message per topic, threads for detail]

### Memos and documents
- [e.g. one-page max, headline conclusion at the top, numbers in tables not prose]

### Presentations
- [e.g. one idea per slide, the title is the takeaway, no bullet lists longer than four]

### LinkedIn / public writing
- [e.g. opens with a first-person scene, closes with a question to peers, no hashtags in the body]

## Rules for the AI when drafting for me

- [e.g. Never pad. If the draft is done in three sentences, stop.]
- [e.g. Never apologize on my behalf unless I've said something was my mistake.]
- [e.g. Give me one strong version, not three options, unless I ask.]
- [e.g. Mark anything you're guessing with [?].]
